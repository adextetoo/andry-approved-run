The closing 42screens the run points at but never draws

Every affordance on all 171 approved plates was read out of the markup and checked against the screens that exist. The approved work is not altered here — this is a register of what sits behind it. 42 screens close every dead end, of which 13 block a journey from completing at all. All 42 have since been drawn.

The onboarding gap you flagged is real and is the first entry: plate #1 is titled Onboarding, slide 2 of 3 and its pager draws three dots, but slides 1 and 3 were never made. The same shape repeats at #11, KYC verification, step 2 of 3, whose own stepper names an Identity step marked done and a Bank step marked pending — neither of which exists as a plate.

The register is closed. The 13 flow-blockers are drawn in the thirteen, and the 29 named destinations in the twenty-nine — both on the Andry design system, with nothing in the approved 171 altered. Every labelled affordance in the run now points at a screen that exists.

The counts below are kept as they were found, so the register still reads as the audit that produced them rather than as a summary written afterwards.

The operator consoles had no frame

The desktop token block had lost its selector upstream, so it began mid-comment with orphaned declarations. A browser reads those as a rule prelude and consumes until the next brace — which belongs to .desk — swallowing the console frame's whole declaration block. All 19 operator plates were rendering with the rail stacked above the main pane.

The KYC stepper labels collided

.kyc__l was defined as a 2px connector line, but every stepper in the product puts its step label inside it — #11, #103 and #132. The text overflowed that box and ran into the heading beneath.

How this was counted

The published run was parsed for every interactive element, and the count was verified against the live DOM: 1,296 buttons and 35 links across 171 plates, all of them inert — no href, no handler, no script on the page. That inertness is expected of static design plates and is not the finding.

The finding is narrower. An affordance counts as a dead end only when its own label names a destination — a document, a list, a sub-page, a next step — and no plate among the 171 depicts that destination. In-place controls (filters, tabs, steppers, segmented switches), system hand-offs (share sheets, telephone numbers, file downloads, the OS camera) and actions that resolve on the plate they sit on are excluded. Each entry below cites the plate and the exact label that raised it.

Where a destination was suspected, the whole corpus was searched for it before the entry was written. Security, Currency & region, Points statement and pedigree certificate each appear exactly once in all 171 plates — on the row that links to them, and nowhere else.

Two judgement calls worth your disagreement

The four legal documents could be one screen. C-10 through C-13 are the same component — a scrolling document with a title, a version date and an accept affordance — carrying four different texts. Drawn as one template with four content states the total falls from 42 to 39. They are listed separately because each is linked from a different moment in the journey and a reviewer needs to see each one land.

Payout account may be one screen or three. C-08, C-28 and C-36 all capture a bank account, but the owner's must match a verified personal identity, the supervisor's is personal and paid on a fixed date, and the ranch's is a company account. If the name-match rule at #90 generalises, this is one screen and the total falls to 40 — or 37 alongside the document viewer. If it does not, three plates are the honest answer.
